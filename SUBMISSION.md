# Devpost submission draft

## Name

Barre Code: Movement Cipher Lab

## Track

Education

## One-line description

Decode movement as computation in a standalone lesson where deterministic rules validate structure and GPT-5.6 Sol optionally explains the already-correct result.

## Inspiration

Computational thinking is often introduced at a keyboard, even though sequence, state, parameters, repetition, debugging, and resolution are already present in movement instruction. Movement Cipher Lab makes those relationships legible without turning a human body into data.

## What it does

Learners arrange six synthetic movement ciphers into a dependency-aware phrase. A deterministic TypeScript compiler checks five structural rules and gives exact repairs. The learner then finds a seeded glitch, changes one bounded quality parameter, and downloads a Learning Receipt. After validation, GPT-5.6 Sol may explain why the structure compiles. If the model is unavailable, deterministic coaching preserves the complete lesson.

## How we built it

The app uses Next.js App Router and TypeScript. Pure modules own the movement schema, validator, scoring, variation rules, and receipt construction. A server-only route revalidates the sequence before calling the OpenAI Responses API. Coaching uses strict structured output validated server-side. The interface provides drag-and-drop plus accessible earlier/later buttons, large touch targets, visible focus, semantic HTML, and reduced-motion support.

## Why the authority boundary matters

The model does not decide correctness, score movement, or observe a learner. It explains a result after deterministic validation. This makes graceful failure part of the educational design: the lesson still compiles, teaches, and produces its receipt when AI is unavailable.

## Built during Build Week

The complete lesson implementation, compiler, glitch challenge, bounded variation, server-only coaching route, deterministic fallback, receipt, responsive interface, tests, and documentation were built during the submission period with Codex.

## Pre-existing research

The movement-as-computation teaching premise builds on Allison Van Cura's earlier Ballet Barre Code and Ballet Bots research. The implementation and synthetic lesson presented here are new. Private research archives and the full MetaCodeography source are excluded.
